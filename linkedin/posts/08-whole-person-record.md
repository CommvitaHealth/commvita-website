Harold is 90. He has dementia with behavioural symptoms, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, and a frailty score of 5.

His GP holds part of his record. The memory service holds another part. The hospital that admitted him in March holds a third. Social care holds a fourth. The pharmacy that reviewed his medicines holds a fifth. None of them is wrong. None of them is the whole thing.

The Whole Person Record puts all of it on one screen, as fourteen tabs over one identity.

Two of those tabs are tinted, and the tint isn’t decoration. Problems and Medications come up red, Frailty amber, because that’s where Harold’s risk sits. A clinician with four minutes goes to the red one first. Tab counts come from the same call that fills the tab, so a tab reading 5 over a table showing 3 can’t happen.

The timeline is where it lands for most people. Annual review at the surgery. Cardiology outpatients. A district nurse dressing a leg ulcer. A CPA review. A support plan raised to fourteen hours a week. A medicines review at the pharmacy. An emergency admission in March. A phone call about metformin. A Care Act assessment in January.

Read down it and the year makes sense. Nobody holding one of those systems can see that shape.

The screens come from the running platform on a demonstration record. In a live deployment the timeline fills from GP Connect, FHIR subscriptions and EPR feeds.
