The 10 Year Health Plan asks for three shifts: analogue to digital, hospital to community, treatment to prevention. Most boards measure them in three separate papers, on three separate cycles, with three separate definitions of progress.

LeftShift Intelligence puts all three on one page, scores each of them, rolls them into one index — and then adjusts that index for who the gains are reaching.

The equity modifier is the part worth arguing over. If your digital shift is being carried by the people who were already easiest to reach, the headline number comes down. The gap is subtracted from the score the board is asked to accept.

Each pillar carries what you’d expect, plus one thing you wouldn’t. Digital counts channel adoption, shared record use, remote monitoring and automation — and digital exclusion risk, which counts against the pillar. Community counts avoided admissions, virtual ward use, two-hour urgent community response, length of stay, discharge to usual place of residence, conveyance rate, and the wait for a social care package.

Ahead of the field on this one, from our own assessment of the module against the systems it competes with — our view, not an independent one. Every system we looked at reports the three shifts as separate progress slides, and none of them lets an equity gap move the headline number.

The page runs on seeded demonstration data and says so in a banner at the top of every tab. The production path computes each indicator from the live operational modules sitting in the same platform.
