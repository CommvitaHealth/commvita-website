A diabetes register is easy to produce and hard to act on. The list comes out, the recall letters go out, and the people who most needed the appointment are the ones who don’t turn up.

So the long-term conditions work starts from the review, not the register.

A structured review is one workflow: the checks that belong to the condition, the results already in, what’s missing, the drugs and when each is next due a look, and the action that follows. It sits on the whole-person record instead of a condition-shaped copy of it. Somebody with diabetes, COPD and heart failure is one person having one conversation — not three registers each wanting their own appointment.

The registers still matter. They drive recall, they feed QOF, and they tell you the size of the problem. What changes is what happens when a name comes up: the review is ready, the gaps are named, and finishing it updates the record everyone else reads.

Then put deprivation over the top, and the call list stops being alphabetical. Who’s overdue, who’s furthest from care, who’s missed twice — that’s a different list from the one the register produces on its own, and it’s the one worth working through on a Tuesday afternoon.
