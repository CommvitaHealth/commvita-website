The most expensive integration engine in any care provider is its own staff.

Nobody buys it that way. It arrives by accident. Primary care runs one system, community another, mental health a third, social care a fourth, the acute trust a fifth. None of them talk. So a nurse reads one screen, writes on a second, phones a third, and assembles in their head a picture that should already have existed.

Then we do the same thing at the top of the building. Board management here, a risk register there, incidents somewhere else — each bought on its own, each maintained on its own, none of them speaking. By the time the pack is printed, the numbers in it have moved.

We keep calling this an integration problem. It’s a design problem. When systems don’t connect, people become the connective tissue, and people are the most costly and error-prone middleware there is. They’re also the ones who leave.

commvita starts from the other end: design for flows, and never make a person the workaround.
