Ask a board for its principal risks and you’ll get a document. Ask it to show you the evidence under one of them and you’ll get a date for a follow-up.

The board assurance framework ought to be the live end of everything an organisation knows about itself. It usually isn’t, for a dull reason: the evidence lives in other systems, so the framework gets rebuilt by hand each quarter from whatever could be gathered in time.

On one platform that stops being necessary. Board management, papers, committees and actions sit alongside the BAF, and the BAF reads from the same risk source the operational and corporate registers read. The portal reads it live. Drill into a principal risk and you’re back down the golden thread to the systemic issue, the theme and the events — the ones reported on wards this month, not the ones that made it into last quarter’s pack.

What changes in the room is the question. It moves from “is this number right?” to “what are we doing about it”, which is the question a board is there for.

This edition gets bought by boards, carries no clinical workflow, and needs no clinical accreditation to get started.
