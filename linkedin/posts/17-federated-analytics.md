“Give us the data and we’ll do the analysis” is how most research requests begin, and it’s a fair part of why they take nine months.

Federated query turns that round. The question travels. The data stays where it is.

Cohort discovery runs across participating sites and returns counts, never records. An OMOP node means the query gets written once against a common data model and is understood wherever it lands. Aggregate only, with small-number suppression, and an extract ledger recording every request and what came back.

Two things this buys you.

Feasibility in an afternoon. “How many people in this region have heart failure, an eGFR under 45 and no ACE inhibitor?” becomes a question you can answer before anyone writes a protocol — which changes which studies get proposed at all.

And a defensible answer when somebody asks where the data went. It didn’t go anywhere. That’s a much shorter conversation with a Caldicott Guardian than a data-sharing agreement for a full extract.

The same node serves board reporting and commissioner analytics. One pipe, governed once.
