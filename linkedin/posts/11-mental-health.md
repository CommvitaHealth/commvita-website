Fragmentation costs most in mental health, because the people it serves are the least able to carry their own history between services.

Someone under a community mental health team also has a GP. Probably a social worker. Sometimes a support package. Often a physical health condition nobody has reviewed in two years. The mental health record holds one part of that. Everything else sits somewhere else.

In commvita, mental health isn’t a bolt-on with its own database. It writes to the same record as everything else. A CPA review lands on the timeline next to the cardiology appointment and the district nurse visit. The medication list is one list, so the antipsychotic and the anticoagulant are in front of the same clinician at the same moment — which is where the physical health gap in serious mental illness gets closed, or doesn’t.

The statutory side is in there too: the Mental Health Services Data Set submission, talking therapies, and mental health records carrying their own access rules. One record can’t mean everybody reads everything, and the platform is built to hold both of those ideas at once.

Demonstrated on seeded data today.
