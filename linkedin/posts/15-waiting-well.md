We treat the waiting list as an administrative object. It’s a cohort of people, and some of them are getting worse while they’re on it.

Waiting Well starts from that. While somebody waits, what can be done that isn’t waiting?

Prehabilitation where it changes the outcome. Optimisation of whatever will complicate the operation — the HbA1c, the blood pressure, the smoking, the weight, the anaemia nobody has chased. Contact that tells people where they are, which is most of what they want. Escalation when their condition moves while they’re in the queue.

Underneath it sits a unified waiting list, which sounds dull and isn’t. One list across the pathways means the person waiting on three of them is one person, counted once, with the interactions visible. Anyone who has tried to reconcile three separate PTLs at month end knows exactly what that’s worth.

And because the record and the operational layer are the same platform, “who on this list has a long-term condition that’s drifting?” is a question you can put to it. That’s the one that decides whether waiting well is a leaflet or a service.

Demonstrated on seeded data.
