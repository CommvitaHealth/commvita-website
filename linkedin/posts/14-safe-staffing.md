Two numbers govern a ward at 7am: who’s on, and who should be. Plenty of organisations can produce the second one monthly and the first one never.

Safe staffing here runs as a daily operational job with the board return falling out of it, which is the right way round.

Planned against actual by shift. Care hours per patient day. And the professional judgement entry, because a ward that’s numerically fine and clinically stretched is a real thing, and a staffing return with nowhere to record it is lying politely.

When there’s a gap, the bank and agency cascade picks it up. Bank first, by rule, with agency as the escalation it was always meant to be, and working time compliance checked as shifts get filled. The interesting output isn’t the fill rate. It’s the pattern underneath — which wards, which shifts, which weeks, and what it cost to cover.

That pattern belongs in front of the board, which is being asked to assure safe staffing under the national quality board guidance while usually only seeing it in summary.

Same platform, so the incident on that ward and the staffing on that shift are one query.
