A district nurse can do eight or ten visits in a day, in houses with no signal, on a phone they’re also using to navigate. Anything needing a desk gets written up at nine that night, from memory.

That’s the real constraint in community nursing, and most software walks straight past it.

The Community Nursing Portal is built for the round instead of for the office. My Day lists the day’s visits and moves them along as they happen — scheduled, en route, on scene, completed — with navigate and call on each one, and a lone-worker check-in, because somebody going into a stranger’s house alone at seven in the evening is a safety question before it’s a data question.

At the visit, Intervene takes the observations — heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, SpO₂, temperature, AVPU, pain nought to ten — and scores NEWS2 there and then with a RAG band. Wound care and medication forms sit next to it. Deterioration gets scored at the bedside rather than back at base, and a rising score can step the person up into a virtual ward without anybody making a phone call.

It works with no signal. Sync is offline-first, so the round carries on when the house doesn’t.

Every screen carries the identity banner — name, demographics, NHS number — so the visit is written against a real person, on the same record the GP and the hospital are reading.

Where this sits today: the portal is a demonstrated surface. The frailty programme, the virtual ward and the shared neighbourhood record underneath it are live.
