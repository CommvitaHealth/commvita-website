A subject access request arrives. Who owns the clock?

In most organisations the answer is a shared mailbox, a spreadsheet and somebody’s memory. The statutory deadline is real and the penalty is real. The tracking is a spreadsheet.

The information governance spine treats this as workflow, because that’s what it is. Records, freedom of information, subject access, the national data opt-out, legitimate access logging, supplier assurance and the Article 30 record of data flows all sit in one place, with the clock running where the work gets done.

Two parts of it get less attention than they deserve.

Legitimate access logging. Not a log you could go and inspect if you had a reason — one the platform writes on every read, hash-chained, so an inappropriate look at a record is findable afterwards and provable once you’ve found it.

And the opt-out, handled as policy the system enforces. Shared care opt-outs sit in a registry built on FHIR Consent with a governed emergency override, so the override leaves a trace of its own.

An inspector’s favourite question is “show me the evidence”. This is what makes that a short conversation.
