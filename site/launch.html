The question

What would you say if it already existed?

A single digital health system that worked at system level — orchestrating flow, and turning it into insight you can act on while it still matters. That also carried the governance, in the same platform and off the same record: incident management, board meetings, information governance, the board assurance framework, regulatory compliance, mandatory training and qualifications, quality impact assessments, financial recovery programme management. And underneath it, a single community-based record that adapts when your structures change. On openEHR record shapes. With transparent, integrated pricing that does not lock you in.

Well — now that’s possible. It’s built.

9 January 2007

The precedent for asking the question at all

At the Macworld Expo in San Francisco, Steve Jobs held up one device and described three: a phone, an iPod, and an internet communicator. The point was never the hardware. It was the observation that three things people already carried had no business being three things.

The same assumption

Health carries three

The record is one system. Flow and operations are another. Governance and assurance are a third, usually a folder of spreadsheets. Nobody chose that arrangement; it accumulated.

The cost of the seams

The programme never finishes

Every seam needs an interface, an assurance case, an infrastructure footprint and a team to keep it alive. The seams cost more than the systems they join.

The tell

People are the integration layer

The clearest sign a stack is wrong is the number of people employed to move data between its parts. No other real-time industry tolerates it, and health should not have to.

What was asked for

Twelve things, one platform

Not a suite with an integration layer between the products. One record, one jurisdiction profile, one audit trail, read by everything above it.

Orchestration

System-level flow orchestration

The health economy as one managed production line, with the binding constraint named and the cost of delay priced against the queue actually holding it — not a dashboard of the same numbers.

Real-time, actionable insight

Insight while it still matters, attached to the action it implies, rather than a report describing what happened three weeks ago.

Governance and assurance

Incident management

PSIRF-aligned, proportionate response, LFPSE reporting, and a duty of candour clock that starts itself.

Board meetings and papers

Agendas, packs, resolutions, voting and an action tracker, on a hash-chained decision trail a regulator can read.

Information governance

DSPT, DPIAs, the ICO breach clock, Caldicott, and the four statutory registers — FOI on the working-day clock, retention and disposal, the asset register with its Article 30 record, and data lineage.

Board assurance framework

Principal risks against strategic objectives, three lines of assurance, and gaps in assurance stated as gaps.

Regulatory compliance management

The regulator, inspection framework, background-check scheme and professional register resolved per jurisdiction — never hardcoded to one country's.

Mandatory training and qualifications

Statutory training, CPD, professional registration and background checks as one credentialling view, with expiry derived rather than typed.

Quality impact assessments

A gate enforced in software: a saving that has not been assessed for its effect on quality, safety, access and equity cannot be approved.

Financial recovery programme management

Targets, schemes, phasing and benefits that are validated with evidence — and cannot be signed off by the person who owns them.

The record, and the terms

A single community-based record

Primary care, community services, mental health, social care, pharmacy and dentistry in one record, that adapts when your structures change because organisation is a dated, evidenced model rather than a column.

Transparent pricing, no lock-in

Published prices, self-hosted in your own tenancy, open standards, and exit designed as a capability rather than written as a clause.

What changes next

The record is about to stop belonging to the organisation

Reported direction of travel: participation in a single patient record moves from voluntary to statutory. If that happens, the question a board asks its supplier changes — from can you build us a good record? to can our record become part of somebody else’s?

Complying

The easy half

A duty can compel you to expose data, in a stated format, within a stated time. That is a delivery problem: hard, expensive, and bounded.

Enabling

The half nobody legislates

Whether the resulting record is usable turns on four things a statute cannot require: resolving which person, surviving reorganisation, attributing every element to its contributor, and establishing the access basis.

What is true today

The claims worth checking first

What “openEHR” means here, precisely

commvita is designed to openEHR’s record shapes and writes a composition to an EHRbase repository on consultation completion, and per-jurisdiction EHRbase containers ship with the platform. That is a real integration and it works against a real clinical data repository.

It is not a native openEHR implementation, and we do not describe it as one. commvita ships 0 archetype definitions, has no AQL query support, and the default repository target in a stock configuration is a mock. A platform that claimed “openEHR-native” on this basis would be overstating it, and a buyer would find out during implementation rather than during procurement.

The reason to say so on a marketing page is simple: the gap is discoverable in about ten minutes by anyone technical, and a claim that fails that test damages every other claim beside it.

Real-time means where the feeds are connected

The production line computes flow, work in progress, queue and the binding constraint from real operational state where the operational feeds are connected. Demand, returns and quality are representative until those feeds connect, and the platform says which it is showing rather than presenting both as live. A demonstration environment is seeded, and is labelled seeded.

The 80% is an ambition for total cost of ownership, worded that way on every commvita page including this one. commvita launches today, so nobody has banked it. What is not an ambition is the published price.

commvita is not a medical device. The platform presents recorded information and supports decisions; clinical decisions remain with the clinician.
