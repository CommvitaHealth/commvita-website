Competitive intelligence: ahead, on par, behind, and why

A register inside the platform, revised every build with AI assistance, rating 440 modules against the products that do the same job. 57 modules are behind at least one product, across 99 behind comparisons, and every one of the 99 says what it’s behind on.

1 A register, updated every build

Most competitive comparisons in health software are a slide made for a bid and never touched again. commvita keeps its comparison as a module inside the platform, one entry per capability, rated against the named products that do the same job, and it is revised as part of every build.

The coverage is calculated on the screen instead of asserted. The module registry holds 629 entries. Twelve of them are cross-cutting capabilities with no screen to compare, such as brute-force lockout or per-record scoping. A hundred and ninety-eight are legacy duplicate entries pointing at the same route as another entry, resolved by route so they aren’t given a fabricated second audit. That leaves 419 distinct routed modules, every one audited, producing 440 audit entries because 21 routes host more than one distinct capability and each is rated on its own. A build check enforces both directions: no routed module without an audit entry, and no entry without a module.

2 The numbers today

There are two counts on the screen and they measure different things, so here they are together. Counting modules: of the 440 entries, 237 are ahead of every product they’re rated against, 146 are on par with at least one and behind none, and 57 are behind at least one. Counting comparisons: each module is rated against several products, giving 1,109 comparisons across 454 named products, of which 742 are ahead, 268 on par and 99 behind.

The 57 and the 99 reconcile like this. Of the 57 modules behind, 23 are behind one product, 28 are behind two, four are behind three and two are behind four. Add those up and you get 99 behind comparisons. The tiles count modules; the breakdown above them counts comparisons, because a module behind two products may be behind them for two different reasons, and the reason is what you plan against.

A module counts as behind if any one product beats it on any one comparison, which is why 57 modules produce 99 behind comparisons. That’s the strict reading, and we use it because the lenient one flatters. The competitor products aren’t named on this page, by house rule; they are named on the platform screen, one by one, with the notes that justify each rating.

3 How “behind” is broken down

Behind is too blunt a word to plan against, so every one of the 99 behind comparisons carries a gap class saying what separates commvita from that particular product. The 99 break down like this, with the number of modules each class touches beside it, because one module can be behind three competitors for one missing capability.

Two rules sit behind that table and are enforced by the build. A rating of not built, not wired or awaiting licence has to say what would close it, because a concession that doesn’t say what would move it is a refusal in disguise. And a rating conceded by design, to a partner or to scope, is forbidden from carrying a “moved by” at all, because there is nothing to move. A hybrid verdict like “ahead but not live” was considered and rejected: it folds two facts into one word and gets shortened to “ahead” the first time it reaches a board pack.

4 How the assessment is made

The audit is written with AI assistance at every build and revised against measurement, and a rating only moves when something measurable changed. Deciding to build a capability moves nothing. A plan is not a capability.

The verdict is honest about deployment. If a commvita surface is seeded and the comparator is a working product, commvita is behind, however good the design, and the gap class records that the gap is deployment. The openEHR column is the clearest example: 204 entries are marked openEHR enabled and none is marked native, because native would mean clinical data persisted as compositions against published archetypes and queryable with the standard query language, and no module claims that today. The tile now says in so many words that enabled isn’t native and that what stands between them is licensed content, not code. The build that took the register to this state also found fifteen places across the platform that advertised a query language commvita doesn’t have, two of them competitive ratings of ahead resting on it, and corrected all fifteen. Twenty-three archetype identifiers the platform names were then checked against the international register: eighteen are present, five are not, and the five are named on the screen instead of dropped.

Claims the register used to make and later withdrew are kept on the entry, with the date, instead of being deleted. A register that only shows what it currently claims can’t answer the question a buyer who read an older deck asks: was this ever asserted, and when did it stop being?

5 The same discipline for how data is used

The competitive register answers “how good is this module?”. A second register answers “is what I’m looking at real?”, and it is measured the same way.

The data transparency register classifies all 422 routed pages from their own source: 90 read the platform, 211 read the platform and carry seeded figures too, and 121 are seeded. The top bar of every page shows the result as a badge. The rule that fails the build is narrow on purpose: a page may understate itself, and may never claim platform data while fetching nothing. Where a page is mixed the register leaves it mixed, because deciding which tile shows which would be a guess.

6 No feature promises

Every founder of commvita has been on the receiving end of a feature promise: the module that was “in the next release” through three contract renewals. We vowed not to do it, and the register is how the vow is enforced on us.

There’s no roadmap column in the competitive audit. A capability is rated on what runs today, and the honest words for what doesn’t run are the seven gap classes above. When something is built it is measured, the rating moves, and the build register records the measurement. If you are shown a rating in a meeting and want to know what it rests on, the entry is on the platform with its notes and its last-verified date, which at this build ranges from June to September 2026.

The honest edges. This is our assessment of our own platform against products we don’t operate, made from public information and our own measurement. It isn’t independent. Four entries carry withdrawn claims. The comparators’ own capabilities move, and an entry last verified in June may be out of date about them. And the page itself is a seeded register in the provenance sense: it’s data typed into the platform and checked by the build, and it isn’t something the platform computes about the market.
