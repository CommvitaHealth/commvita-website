Competitive intelligence: ahead, on par, behind, and why

A register inside the platform, revised every build with AI assistance, rating 445 capabilities against the products that do the same job. 175 are ahead of everything they’re compared with, 99 are level with at least one, and 171 are behind at least one. Every rating of behind says what it’s behind on, and why.

1 A register, updated every build

Most competitive comparisons in health software are a slide made for a bid and never touched again. commvita keeps its comparison as a module inside the platform, one entry per capability, rated against the named products that do the same job, and it is revised as part of every build.

The coverage is worked out on the screen instead of asserted. The module registry holds 634 entries. Twelve of them are cross-cutting capabilities with no screen to compare, such as brute-force lockout or per-record scoping. A hundred and ninety-eight are older duplicate entries pointing at the same route as another entry, resolved by route so they aren’t given a made-up second audit. That leaves 424 distinct routed modules, every one audited, and 445 audit entries, because 21 routes hold more than one distinct capability and each is rated on its own. The build checks both directions: no routed module without an audit entry, and no entry without a module.

2 The numbers today

Counted at build B-973. There are two counts on the screen and they measure different things, so here they are together. Counting modules: of the 445 entries, 175 are ahead of every product they’re rated against, 99 are level with at least one and behind none, and 171 are behind at least one. Counting comparisons: each module is rated against several products, 1,126 comparisons in all, against 1,033 named products from 456 suppliers. Of those, 524 come out ahead, 197 level and 405 behind.

VerdictWhat it means for a module
Aheadcommvita is ahead of every product the module is compared with. One product doing better on one point is enough to lose it.
On parLevel with at least one product and behind none.
BehindBehind at least one product on at least one point. The entry says what it’s behind on and what would close the gap.

The 171 and the 405 reconcile like this. Of the 171 modules behind, 29 are behind one product, 69 are behind two, 58 behind three, 13 behind four and two behind six. That adds up to 405 comparisons where commvita is behind. The tiles count modules; the breakdown above them counts comparisons, because a module behind two products may be behind them for two different reasons, and the reason is what you plan against.

A module counts as behind if any one product beats it on any one point, which is why 171 modules produce 405 comparisons where commvita is behind. That’s the strict reading, and we use it because the lenient one flatters. The competitor products aren’t named on this page, by house rule. They’re named on the platform screen, one by one, with the notes behind each rating.

What a rating rests on

Every rating carries a note saying why. Three of them, with the products’ names taken out:

Ahead · /jurisdiction-agent
A country’s rules as configurationIdentifier schemes, terminology and legal provisions are versioned, source-cited configuration that a customer can draft, check and sign off themselves. The products it’s compared with handle that as professional-services work or as country versions their supplier builds.
On par · /condition-advice
Ahead on one point, level overallcommvita is ahead on one narrow point: a rule that won’t send special-category advice over the wrong channel. The other product is a deeper and far more widely used messaging service. Level is the honest call.
Behind · /single-patient-record
Built, and not on a live networkThe parts that find and exchange a person’s records across organisations are built against the real record. What commvita doesn’t have is a live national exchange network, and the products it’s compared with run at scale. The gap is deployment.

3 Why the numbers moved in October

Until now this page said 237 ahead, 146 on par and 57 behind. Most of the change since comes from one check, made in October at build B-855.

The check looked at every rating that put commvita ahead of, or level with, a working product where commvita’s own screen still shows demonstration data. There were 275 of them. Each one was asked a single question: does the specific thing this rating claims rest on something real that a person can use on the platform today? Being cheaper, open or self-hosted didn’t count on its own, because those are commercial positions and a rating has to rest on a capability. None of the 275 passed. 272 moved to behind. The other three were rows with no competitor named at all, and they’re now counted apart.

So most of the new ratings of behind don’t say a competitor got better. They say that a screen showing commvita’s design over demonstration data isn’t a working product, and a working product beats it. Of the 272, 237 are now classed as not built, because nothing real sits behind the screen yet. Twenty-seven are built but not wired: the server holds the real thing and the screen doesn’t use it. Every one says what would move it back. The diabetes screen, for example, moves when the server holds a diabetes register with foot risk, retinal screening grade, structured education and remission records, and the screen reads them.

The check covered screens that show only demonstration data. Ratings on other screens are our assessment, made the same way as before. Of the 524 comparisons where commvita is ahead, 138 are on screens that read only live data, 371 on screens that read live data and still carry some demonstration panels, and 15 on pages the platform’s data register doesn’t classify. None is on a screen that shows only demonstration data. Smaller changes in other builds make up the rest: five capabilities added to the register, and a few ratings moved either way as features were built or checked again.

4 How “behind” is broken down

Behind is too blunt a word to plan against, so each of the 405 comparisons carries a gap class saying what separates commvita from that particular product. Here they are, with the number of modules each class touches beside it, because one module can be behind three products for one missing piece.

Comparisons · modulesGap classWhat it means
310 · 127Not builtDesigned and not yet built. That includes a screen with nothing real behind it: no server capability, or one that returns fixed values or keeps its data only in memory. Since October this is where most screens showing demonstration data sit.
28 · 17Not deployed or certifiedImplemented, and not yet run at scale, certified or exercised live. The shared record above is an example.
24 · 9Built, not wiredThe server holds the real thing and the screen doesn’t call it yet. A wiring task.
22 · 16Design gapThe capability isn’t in the platform’s design. One example is access control that follows data lineage record by record, which goes further than commvita’s organisation-scoped permissions.
9 · 5Awaiting licenceBuilt, and blocked on licensed content or a credential. Using the openEHR template library is one: it’s a commercial decision, and no code would change it.
8 · 6Out of scope by designThe other product’s role is one commvita chooses not to occupy. Conceded, and never to be moved.
4 · 4Upstream partnerConceded to a supplier commvita partners with. Also never to be moved.

Two rules sit behind that table and are enforced by the build. A rating of not built, not wired or awaiting licence has to say what would close it, because a concession that doesn’t say what would move it is a refusal in disguise. And a rating conceded by design, to a partner or to scope, is forbidden from carrying a “moved by” at all, because there is nothing to move. A hybrid verdict like “ahead but not live” was considered and rejected: it folds two facts into one word and gets shortened to “ahead” the first time it reaches a board pack.

5 How the assessment is made

The audit is written with AI assistance at every build and revised against measurement, and a rating only moves when something measurable changed. Deciding to build a capability moves nothing. A plan is not a capability.

The verdict is honest about what’s real. If a commvita screen shows demonstration data and the comparator is a working product, commvita is behind however good the design, and the gap class says what’s missing: not built when nothing real sits behind the screen, built but not wired when something does. The openEHR column is the clearest example: 204 entries are marked openEHR enabled and none is marked native, because native would mean clinical data persisted as compositions against published archetypes and queryable with the standard query language, and no module claims that today. The tile now says in so many words that enabled isn’t native and that what stands between them is licensed content, not code. The build that took the register to this state also found fifteen places across the platform that advertised a query language commvita doesn’t have, two of them competitive ratings of ahead resting on it, and corrected all fifteen. Twenty-three archetype identifiers the platform names were then checked against the international register: eighteen are present, five are not, and the five are named on the screen instead of dropped.

Claims the register used to make and later withdrew are kept on the entry, with the date, instead of being deleted. A register that only shows what it currently claims can’t answer the question a buyer who read an older deck asks: was this ever asserted, and when did it stop being?

6 The same discipline for how data is used

The competitive register answers “how good is this module?”. A second register answers “is what I’m looking at real?”, and it is measured the same way.

The data transparency register classifies all 427 routed pages from their own source: 97 read the platform, 232 read the platform and carry seeded figures too, and 98 are seeded. The top bar of every page shows the result as a badge. The rule that fails the build is narrow on purpose: a page may understate itself, and may never claim platform data while fetching nothing. Where a page is mixed the register leaves it mixed, because deciding which tile shows which would be a guess.

7 No feature promises

Every founder of commvita has been on the receiving end of a feature promise: the module that was “in the next release” through three contract renewals. We vowed not to do it, and the register is how the vow is enforced on us.

There’s no roadmap column in the competitive audit. A capability is rated on what runs today, and the honest words for what doesn’t run are the seven gap classes above. When something is built it is measured, the rating moves, and the build register records the measurement. If you are shown a rating in a meeting and want to know what it rests on, the entry is on the platform with its notes and its last-verified date, which at this build ranges from June to September 2026.

The honest edges. This is our assessment of our own platform against products we don’t operate, made from public information and our own measurement. It isn’t independent. Three entries carry withdrawn claims. The ratings that moved in October still show their earlier last-verified dates, so a date alone won’t tell you whether that check reached an entry. The comparators’ own capabilities move, and an entry last verified in June may be out of date about them. And the page itself is a seeded register in the provenance sense: it’s data typed into the platform and checked by the build, and it isn’t something the platform computes about the market.
